The care tracking system lets you log every care action for every plant in your collection. It's designed for mobile use — log care at the shelf, not from memory later.

## Care types

The app tracks five care actions, each with a quick-action button on the plant detail page:

| Action | Default threshold | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watering | 7 days | Logs the event, updates the plant's "last watered" date |
| Fertilizing | 14 days | Lets you select the fertilizer type used (stored as structured data, not a note) |
| Grooming | 7 days | Covers removing dead leaves, spent blooms, cleaning the crown |
| Repotting | None | Logs the event and lets you update the pot size on the plant record |
| Treatment | None | Lets you select the pest or disease treatment type used |

The watering, fertilizing, and grooming thresholds are the defaults — you can change any of them in **Settings → Care**.

## Care status indicators

Each plant displays a care status based on time elapsed since the last action:

**Good** (green) — Care was performed recently. No action needed.

**Soon** (yellow) — Approaching the overdue threshold (within 80%). A gentle nudge.

**Overdue** (red) — The threshold has been exceeded. The plant appears in the dashboard's Today section.

Plants that have never been cared for in a given category default to Overdue.

## Care log

Every care action creates a timestamped entry in the **Care Log**. Access it from the app menu to see all actions across your collection, or from a specific plant's detail page to see that plant's history. The log supports filtering by both plant and care type, and a calendar view that lays your care history out by day.

## Tips

**Log as you go.** The quick-action buttons are intentionally simple so you can tap them while standing at the shelf. Don't batch-log later — you'll forget things.

**Use fertilizer type tracking.** Over time, this structured data will let you correlate fertilizer choices with bloom performance.

**Check the dashboard regularly.** The Today section catches plants you might have overlooked.

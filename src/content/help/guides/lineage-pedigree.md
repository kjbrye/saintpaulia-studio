The Lineage & Pedigree Tracker records how your plants are related — their parents, generation, and connection to your breeding program.

## Why track lineage

For serious breeders, knowing a plant's parentage is essential. Lineage data helps you avoid inbreeding by knowing which plants share ancestors, predict traits based on what parents and grandparents exhibited, document your work for sharing with other growers or registering new cultivars, and trace back problems if a genetic issue shows up in offspring.

## Lineage data

Every plant in your collection can carry lineage information:

**Pod parent** and **Pollen parent** — The two parents of the plant.

**Generation label** — F1, F2, etc., automatically calculated based on parentage.

**Cross reference** — A link back to the Breeding Tracker cross that produced this plant.

When you add offspring through the Breeding Tracker's completion flow, all of this is pre-filled.

## Clones versus offspring

The lineage page distinguishes two kinds of relationship. A plant grown from a **leaf cutting** is a clone — it shares its source's genome — and appears in a separate clones ribbon rather than as a descendant. A plant grown from a **cross** is genetically new offspring and appears in the descendant tree. Keeping the two apart means your pedigree reflects genetics, not just where a plant came from.

## Cross notation

Saintpaulia Studio uses the standard botanical × (multiplication sign) for cross notation, following established conventions in the African violet community:

`Pod Parent Name × Pollen Parent Name`

The pod parent is always listed first. This matters because some traits are maternally inherited.

## Generation tracking

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| F1 | Direct offspring of two unrelated parent cultivars |
| F2 | Offspring of two F1 plants, or a selfed F1 |
| F3+ | Successive generations |

Generation labels indicate how far removed a plant is from the original cross and what level of trait variation to expect.

## Viewing pedigree

From any plant's detail page, you can view its pedigree information showing parents and (where data exists) grandparents. This builds up over time as you record more crosses and add more offspring.

## Tips

**Be consistent with cultivar names.** Lineage tracking depends on exact name matches. "Rob's Vanilla Trail" and "Robs Vanilla trail" won't connect automatically.

**Record lineage for purchased plants** if you know the parentage. Many specialty growers provide this information.

**Start tracking early.** The more data you enter now, the richer your pedigree view becomes over time.

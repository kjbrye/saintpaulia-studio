African violets are commonly propagated from leaf cuttings. A single leaf can produce multiple plantlets over the course of weeks to months. The Propagation Tracker lets you document each step of this process so you know what's growing, where it came from, and how it's progressing.

## Starting a propagation project

To begin, select the **source plant** you're taking a leaf from, record the date the cutting was taken, and add any notes about the cutting (leaf condition, rooting medium, etc.). The propagation project then lives as its own trackable item, linked back to the source plant.

## Stages

As your cutting develops, advance the project through these stages:

| Stage | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Cutting | The leaf has been taken and is forming a callus at the cut |
| Rooting | Roots emerge from the callus and lengthen |
| Plantlets | Baby crowns sprout at the base of the parent leaf |
| Potted | Separated plantlets are growing on in their own pots |
| Established | A plantlet is a mature plant, ready to join your collection |

## Adding offspring to your collection

Once a plantlet reaches the **Potted** stage and is growing steadily, you can add it directly to your Plant Library as a new plant. The app pre-fills the parent and lineage information so each new plant has a clear record of where it came from. Because a leaf cutting produces a clone, the new plant is linked to its source as a clone rather than as bred offspring.

## Tips

**Take photos at each stage.** Progress photos help you learn what healthy development looks like and are satisfying to look back on.

**Note your rooting medium.** Track whether you used water, perlite, sphagnum, or soil. Over time you'll discover what works best for you.

**Don't rush separation.** Most growers wait until plantlets have 3–4 leaves before separating them.

Leaf cuttings are the most common way to propagate African violets. A single healthy leaf can produce several plantlets over a few months.

## Take the cutting

Select a healthy, mature leaf from the source plant and cut the stem at an angle.

## Start the project in the app

Go to the Propagation Tracker and create a new project, selecting the source plant and recording the date. The project starts at the **Cutting** stage.

## Advance through the stages

Update the project as the cutting develops:

- **Cutting** → the leaf is forming a callus at the cut.
- **Rooting** → roots emerge from the callus and lengthen.
- **Plantlets** → baby crowns sprout at the base of the parent leaf.
- **Potted** → you've separated the plantlets into their own pots.
- **Established** → a plantlet has matured into a plant ready to join your collection.

## Add the new plants

When plantlets are potted individually and growing steadily, add each one to your Plant Library. Lineage data links them back to the source plant automatically — as clones, since a leaf cutting shares the source's genome rather than creating something new.
